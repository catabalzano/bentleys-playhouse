A dog doesn't have to be bleeding to be in danger. Dogs left outside day and night, tied to a fence, locked in a crate in the yard or kept on a balcony suffer every day, and in South Florida's heat, rain and storms, it can turn deadly fast.

If the dog is in immediate danger, stop here and follow our [emergency steps](/get-help/emergency/).

## Signs a Dog May Be Neglected

- Kept outside all the time, day and night.
- Tied or chained to a fence, pole, tree or stake.
- Locked in a crate or cage outdoors, or on a balcony.
- Left out in the rain, storms or full sun with no real shelter or shade.
- No food or water you can see, or a bowl that's always empty, tipped over or dirty.
- Very thin: you can see the ribs, hip bones or spine.
- Sitting or lying in its own waste, with sores, hair loss, a matted coat or overgrown nails.
- Never walked or let out to move around.

**A dog that isn't skinny can still be neglected.** Florida law covers more than food. Keeping an animal confined without water, or without "wholesome exercise and change of air," is a crime under Section 828.13, and Miami-Dade's tethering law applies no matter how well fed the dog looks. A dog can look healthy and still be living in conditions that are illegal.

## Cases We've Seen

These situations aren't rare. Some of the cases Bentley's Playhouse has shared have gone viral, including Dior, a French bulldog kept on a balcony, and [Chase](/pledge/chase-a2828453/), a pit bull kept in a small cage outdoors. Screenshots from Google Maps appeared to show Chase in that cage as far back as late 2025. When he was finally surrendered to Miami-Dade Animal Services, his exam noted he was underweight, with skin irritation, hair loss, inflamed paws and eye problems.

Cases like these move faster when neighbors report early, keep reporting and keep evidence.

## What the Law Says

**Florida Statute 828.12, animal cruelty.** It's a crime to deprive an animal "of necessary sustenance or shelter" or to treat it in a cruel or inhumane manner. That's a first-degree misdemeanor with a fine up to $5,000. If it causes a cruel death or "excessive or repeated infliction of unnecessary pain or suffering," it's aggravated animal cruelty, a third-degree felony with a fine up to $10,000.

**Florida Statute 828.13, confinement and abandonment.** It's a crime to confine an animal and fail to give it enough "good and wholesome food and water," or to keep any animal in an enclosure "without wholesome exercise and change of air." That's a first-degree misdemeanor with a fine up to $5,000.

**Trooper's Law, Section 828.13(4).** Named after Trooper, a bull terrier a Florida Highway Patrol trooper found tied to a fence in rising water on Interstate 75 before Hurricane Milton in 2024. Since Oct. 1, 2025, anyone who restrains a dog outside during a natural disaster and then abandons it commits a third-degree felony, with a fine up to $10,000. A natural disaster here means a hurricane, tropical storm or tornado warning from the National Weather Service, or a mandatory or voluntary evacuation order.

**Miami-Dade County Code Section 5-21, tethering.** In Miami-Dade, a dog may only be tethered outdoors if all of these are true: the owner is outside with the dog and can see it, the dog has water, shelter and dry ground, and it's not extreme weather, which includes extreme heat, thunderstorms, tropical storms and hurricanes. Other rules include a buckle collar or harness at least 1 inch wide, a tether at least five times the dog's body length, no puppies under 6 months and no sick or injured dogs. Section 5-4 also bans depriving an animal of food, water or shelter.

## What to Say to Animal Services

Call **311** or **305-468-5900**. Be calm, specific and brief. For example:

> I'm reporting a dog that's being neglected at [exact address or cross streets]. The dog is [tied to a fence / in a cage in the yard / on a balcony] and has been kept outside [since when, how often you've seen it]. Right now it's [raining / in full sun, about 90 degrees] and I can't see any water or shelter. Under Florida Statute 828.13 and Miami-Dade County Code Section 5-21, keeping a dog tethered or confined outside like this, without water, shelter and exercise, isn't legal. I have photos and videos with dates. Can I get a report number, and can someone do a welfare check today?

## What to Say to the Police

Call **911** if it's happening right now, or **305-SHERIFF** if it isn't. For example:

> I'd like to report animal cruelty at [exact address]. A dog is [describe what you see] and has been kept this way since [date]. I believe this violates Florida Statutes 828.12 and 828.13. I already reported it to Animal Services on [date], report number [number]. I'd like a police report filed. Can I get the case number and the officer's name?

During a hurricane, tropical storm or tornado warning, or an evacuation order, add: "This dog is tied up outside during a natural disaster, which is a felony under Trooper's Law, Section 828.13(4)."

## How to Gather Evidence

The more you can prove the dog is **always** kept this way, the easier it is to show neglect, and not a one-time thing.

- **Film and photograph from a public place** or your own property. Never trespass.
- **Repeat it.** Morning, afternoon and night; weekdays and weekends; rain and heat. A few weeks of photos are far stronger than one.
- **Show the conditions:** the tether or cage, the lack of shade or shelter, the empty or missing water bowl, the dog's body.
- **Show where it is:** one wide shot that includes the house, building or street so the location is clear.
- **Keep the originals.** Don't crop or filter them. The date and time in the file matter.
- **Check Google Maps Street View history.** Older images can show the dog was kept the same way months or years ago, as they did for Chase.
- **Keep a simple log:** date, time, weather, what you saw and every call you made.

## Why Report Numbers Matter

Every call to 311 and to the police should end with a report number. Write them all down with the date and who you spoke to, and add your Hotline Report ID. Together they prove that Animal Services and the police were told, when they were told, and how many times. If nothing is done, that record is what holds the agencies accountable, whether you go to the State Attorney's Office, the media or social media.

## Sharing It Online

Posting can help a dog get seen, but stick to facts you saw and recorded. Don't threaten anyone, don't post other people's personal information, and report to the agencies first. You can also [send the case to us](/submit-a-tip/), and we'll review it.
