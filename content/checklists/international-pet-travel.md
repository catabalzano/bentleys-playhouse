intro: Work top to bottom and start as early as you can. Rules change often, so confirm each step with USDA APHIS, the CDC and your airline.
groups:
  - title: 3 to 6 months before (longer for Japan or Hawaii)
    items:
      - Looked up my destination's rules on the APHIS pet travel site
      - Found a USDA-accredited veterinarian who does international certificates
      - Confirmed my dog has an ISO microchip (15 digits) and noted the number
      - Got the rabies vaccine after the microchip (at least 21 days before arrival for the EU or UK)
      - Started the rabies titer test and waiting period, if my destination requires one
      - Checked whether any country on my trip is on the CDC high-risk rabies list
      - Picked an airline that accepts my dog's size and breed, and booked my dog's spot
  - title: About 1 month before
    items:
      - Read my airline's pet rules, fees and health certificate timing
      - Bought a carrier that fits under the seat, or an IATA-compliant crate
      - Started getting my dog used to the carrier or crate
      - Booked the health certificate vet visit inside my destination's time window
      - Asked my vet to do the CDC Certification of U.S.-Issued Rabies Vaccination if I'm visiting a high-risk country
      - Confirmed my dog will be at least 6 months old when we come back to the U.S.
  - title: The final 10 days
    items:
      - Vet exam done and health certificate issued
      - Vet submitted the certificate to APHIS (VEHCS) and I paid the endorsement fee
      - Received the endorsed certificate (original ink copy if my destination needs it)
      - High-risk trip only, CDC U.S.-issued rabies form endorsed by USDA before departure
      - Tapeworm treatment booked 24 to 120 hours before arrival (UK, Ireland, Finland, Malta, Norway)
      - Mexico only, screwworm inspection certificate issued within 5 days of travel
  - title: Travel day
    items:
      - Endorsed health certificate, vaccine records and permits in my carry-on
      - Photos of every document saved on my phone
      - Leash, water, bowl, treats, poop bags and a familiar blanket packed
      - Arrived early for pet check-in
      - Ready to take my dog out of the carrier at security
  - title: Coming home to the U.S.
    items:
      - Filled out a CDC Dog Import Form for each dog and saved the receipt
      - Dog's microchip number matches all my paperwork
      - Endorsed CDC rabies form with me if we were in a high-risk country
      - Receipt ready to show the airline and CBP on arrival
      - Arrival airport or crossing matches the one on my receipt
  - title: Bringing a dog from abroad (rescue or adoption)
    items:
      - Checked whether the dog's country is high-risk for rabies
      - Foreign rabies vaccination and microchip form signed and government-endorsed
      - Rabies titer from a CDC-approved lab, drawn at least 28 days before entry
      - Reservation made at a CDC-registered animal care facility
      - Flight booked into the airport where that facility is located
      - Screwworm certificate, if the dog comes from an affected region
  - title: After arrival
    items:
      - Checked my dog for stress, dehydration or injury and called my vet if worried
      - Updated my microchip registry with my current phone and address
      - Saved all travel paperwork for next time
      - Set a reminder for the next rabies booster
