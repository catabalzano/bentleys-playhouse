The ASPCA describes a **3-3-3 guideline** for new dogs. Think of it as a loose map, not a schedule. Dogs coming out of hard situations often need longer, and that's normal.

## The first 3 days: decompress

Your dog may be nervous, overwhelmed or not interested in food or play. Keep things quiet. Stick to a simple routine, give them a safe space like a crate or a quiet room and don't force interaction or invite lots of visitors.

## The first 3 weeks: learn the routine

Your dog starts to understand when meals, walks and bedtime happen, and more of their personality shows. Some behavior you didn't see at first may appear. Start gentle, reward-based training, and keep the routine steady.

## The first 3 months: feel at home

Trust grows. Keep learning what your dog loves, what worries them and how they show it. Keep building the bond with walks, play and calm time together.

## Helpful habits

- One consistent routine for meals, potty breaks and sleep.
- A safe spot your dog can retreat to, where no one bothers them.
- Short training sessions with treats and praise.
- A vet visit soon after adoption.
- Patience with accidents and setbacks. They're part of the process.

If you're struggling, reach out to the rescue you adopted from, or see [finding a humane trainer](/resources/finding-a-humane-trainer/).
