Taking your dog to London, Paris or Rome is very doable, but it runs on paperwork and precise timing. Great Britain and the European Union each have their own rules, and since Brexit they no longer share one system. A missed tapeworm window or a certificate endorsed one day too early can mean your dog is refused entry or held in quarantine at your expense. This guide explains how the rules work, why the UK is so careful, and the steps for the trips Miami families ask us about most.

## Why the UK is so strict

### A rabies-free island

Defra's rabies control strategy says Great Britain and Ireland have been free of rabies since the disease was eradicated in land animals in 1922. Rare bat lyssaviruses exist in some British bats, but Defra notes these do not affect the country's rabies-free status, which is based on freedom from classical rabies. The same strategy says the highest risk of rabies reaching Great Britain would be an infected animal brought in illegally. That is why the border checks on pets are so careful.

### From six months of quarantine to the Pet Travel Scheme

According to a Defra impact assessment, a mandatory six-month quarantine for pets entering the UK was introduced in the late 19th century. The Pet Travel Scheme (often called PETS) launched as a pilot in England in February 2000. It let dogs and cats from EU countries and certain other countries skip quarantine if they met other disease-control measures, such as microchipping and rabies vaccination.

From 1 January 2012 the UK aligned its rules with the standard EU pet movement controls. Defra's assessment says rabies blood tests and mandatory tick treatment were dropped for most travelers, while tapeworm treatment was kept, with a wider window of 24 to 120 hours before arrival.

### What happens if the rules are not met

GOV.UK says a pet that does not meet the rules may be put into quarantine for up to 4 months, or refused entry if you traveled by sea, and you are responsible for any fees or charges. A pet that fails the checks may have to go into quarantine, be sent back to the country it came from, or be put down. Quarantine must be with a carrier and premises authorised by Defra, and you pay all costs. GOV.UK also notes that since 8 September 2025, pets that need rabies quarantine can no longer arrive at Portsmouth port.

## Getting into Great Britain from the U.S., step by step

Great Britain means England, Scotland and Wales. Northern Ireland follows different rules (see below). The U.S. is a "listed" country, and GOV.UK says dogs coming from the USA need a Great Britain pet health certificate. A U.S. traveler cannot use an EU pet passport or an EU animal health certificate for this route.

1. **Microchip first.** Your dog needs an ISO 11784/11785 microchip, implanted before or on the same day as the rabies vaccine. GOV.UK says a pet whose chip cannot be read on arrival will be refused entry or put into quarantine.
2. **Rabies vaccination, then wait 21 days.** After a primary rabies vaccination, your dog must wait at least 21 full days before entering Great Britain. Boosters given on time do not restart the clock. APHIS says the vaccine must be given on the same day as, or after, the microchip is implanted.
3. **Choose the right health certificate.** APHIS explains that if you (or a person you designate) travel within 5 days of your dog, you use the non-commercial certificate. If no one travels within that 5-day window, you need a commercial certificate, which has much tighter timing: the vet's exam and APHIS endorsement must both happen within 48 hours of departure.
4. **Get the certificate issued and endorsed.** A USDA Accredited Veterinarian completes the certificate, and USDA APHIS must endorse it. For the non-commercial certificate, APHIS says it must be endorsed within 10 days of your dog arriving in the UK. If your dog arrives by sea, the 10 days is extended by the length of the sea journey.
5. **Tapeworm treatment (dogs only).** A vet must treat your dog for the *Echinococcus multilocularis* tapeworm with praziquantel or an equivalent, no less than 24 hours and no more than 120 hours (5 days) before you arrive. The vet records the product, date, time, stamp and signature on the certificate. Build your flight times around this window, including delays.
6. **Fill in the declaration.** GOV.UK requires a declaration form for non-commercial movements.
7. **Book an approved route.** Outside travel from Ireland, pets must enter Great Britain on an approved route and company. GOV.UK says pets have to travel as cargo on a plane unless you are on a chartered private plane or traveling with a guide or assistance dog. Approved routes can change or run only in certain seasons, so check the official list before you book. Your airline may also ask for a vet's fit-to-fly letter.

### What to expect on arrival

If your dog lands at Heathrow, the Heathrow Animal Reception Centre, run by the City of London Corporation, checks non-commercial Pet Travel Scheme arrivals for compliance. It is the airport's live animal border control post and travellers' point of entry, and owners can nominate a UK clearing agent to help with the import. Keep the original endorsed certificate with your dog's paperwork.

### Number of pets, rescues and commercial moves

GOV.UK says your movement counts as commercial, under the "Balai" import rules, if you are selling, rehoming or transferring ownership of the pet, bringing more than 5 pets (unless for a competition or similar event), or if the pet arrives more than 5 days before or after you. This matters for rescuers: flying an adopted dog to a new family in Britain is a transfer of ownership, not a holiday. GOV.UK also says banned dog breeds cannot enter without a valid Certificate of Exemption.

## Leaving Great Britain for the EU

Brexit changed this route. GOV.UK says that if you live in England, Scotland or Wales, you cannot use a pet passport to enter the EU, even one issued in the EU. The British Veterinary Association reported that from 22 April 2026, GB residents can no longer use EU-issued pet passports for EU trips, closing a loophole many owners had relied on. Every GB resident now needs an animal health certificate (AHC) for each trip.

1. **Microchip and rabies vaccination**, with the same 21-day wait after a primary vaccine.
2. **Get an AHC from an official veterinarian (OV).** Take your dog to the vet with proof of microchipping date and vaccination history. The AHC must be used to enter the EU within 10 days of being issued.
3. **Know how long it lasts.** GOV.UK currently says the AHC is valid for 6 months of onward travel within the EU after you enter, and for 6 months for re-entry to Great Britain. You need a new AHC each time you travel from Great Britain to the EU.
4. **Tapeworm, if needed.** GOV.UK lists Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland (unless you hold a Northern Ireland pet travel document) and Norway as needing tapeworm treatment for dogs, 24 to 120 hours before arrival.
5. **Five pets maximum.** GOV.UK says you cannot take more than 5 pets in a private vehicle to an EU country, with exceptions for events and commercial exports.

**Northern Ireland residents** may still use EU pet passports. For trips from Great Britain to Northern Ireland, GOV.UK says you need a free Northern Ireland pet travel document, which lasts as long as you own the pet. With it, your dog needs a microchip but not a rabies vaccine or tapeworm treatment. You scan the chip at the port before leaving by ferry, or notify DAERA at least 10 working days ahead if flying.

**A change to watch:** the UK government's explainer on the May 2025 UK-EU summit says a planned sanitary and phytosanitary agreement would replace the per-trip AHC with a multi-use pet passport for EU travel. No start date was given, and as of October 2026 GOV.UK still requires an AHC for each trip.

## Coming back into Great Britain

GOV.UK says a dog returning from an EU country can use a pet passport issued in an EU country, an AHC issued in Great Britain in the last 6 months, a Great Britain pet health certificate, or a pet passport issued in Great Britain before 1 January 2021. You must still use an approved route, and your dog needs tapeworm treatment 24 to 120 hours before arrival, unless you are coming directly from Finland, Ireland, Northern Ireland, Malta or Norway. For short trips of 5 days or less, GOV.UK advises treating your dog again within 28 days of getting home.

## Traveling around Europe with your dog

### EU pet passports and EU health certificates

The European Commission describes the EU pet passport as the document for pets moving between EU countries. An authorised vet issues it and records the microchip, rabies vaccination and any tapeworm treatment. Since 22 April 2026, EU pet travel falls under Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/131, which replaced the earlier rules in Regulation (EU) No 576/2013. The Commission says passports issued under the older rules before 22 April 2026 remain valid.

Dogs arriving from outside the EU, including from the U.S. and Great Britain, need an EU animal health certificate. The Commission says it is valid for 10 days from issue for entry, and then for a total of 6 months of onward travel within the EU from the date of the entry checks. Owners also sign a written declaration that the move is non-commercial.

### Rules every traveler should know

- **Age and timing:** dogs must be at least 12 weeks old when vaccinated against rabies, and the vaccine becomes valid 21 days after the primary dose.
- **Points of entry:** dogs from outside the EU must pass through a travellers' point of entry designated by each EU country.
- **Number of pets:** the maximum for non-commercial movement is 5 in a single vehicle.
- **Owner travel:** if you cannot travel with your dog, an authorised person may, but you must travel within 5 days before or after the dog.
- **Rabies blood test:** the EU requires a rabies antibody test (at least 0.5 IU/ml, taken at least 90 days before the certificate is issued) only for pets coming from countries not on its approved lists. Check APHIS for your exact destination.
- **Tapeworm countries:** dogs entering Finland, Ireland, Malta, Northern Ireland or Norway must be treated with a praziquantel product 24 to 120 hours before entry, recorded by the vet.

### Trains, tunnels and ferries

- **Eurostar:** pets are not allowed on trains to and from London. Eurostar does allow pets on its other routes between Belgium, France, Germany and the Netherlands, with some exceptions, and guide and assistance animals have their own process.
- **Eurotunnel LeShuttle:** your dog stays in the car with you. LeShuttle says to check in at pet reception at least 1 hour and no more than 2 hours before departure, with an AHC issued within 10 days, a working microchip and a rabies certificate. It allows up to 5 pets per vehicle.
- **Ferries:** only approved sea routes and companies can carry pets into Great Britain, and GOV.UK publishes the current list.

## From the U.S. to the EU, step by step

1. **Microchip** your dog before the rabies vaccine.
2. **Rabies vaccinate** and wait at least 21 days after a primary vaccination.
3. **Pick the certificate.** APHIS says the non-commercial EU health certificate applies if the owner travels within 5 days of the dog with five or fewer animals. Otherwise you need the commercial certificate, which must be issued within 48 hours of travel.
4. **Get it endorsed.** A USDA-accredited vet completes the EU health certificate, and APHIS must endorse it within 10 days of your dog's arrival in the EU.
5. **Tapeworm treatment** if you are heading to Finland, Ireland, Malta or Norway.
6. **Arrive through a travellers' point of entry**, then use the certificate for up to 6 months of travel within the EU, as APHIS and the Commission both state.

## Before you book

This page is general information, not legal or veterinary advice, and pet travel rules change often. Several changed in 2025 and 2026 alone. Before every trip, confirm the current requirements with GOV.UK and APHIS (the UK's Animal and Plant Health Agency), USDA APHIS for your destination, and your airline, ferry or train company. Start planning months ahead, especially if your dog has not yet been microchipped and vaccinated.
